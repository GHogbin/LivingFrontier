LIVING FRONTIER - WILDERNESS TRADE NETWORK FORGE BETA
Version 0.2.0-beta.8
Minecraft Java 1.20.1 / Forge 47.4.0 / Java 17

INSTALL
1. Install Forge 47.4.0 for Minecraft 1.20.1:
   https://files.minecraftforge.net/net/minecraftforge/forge/index_1.20.1.html
2. Create or select a separate Minecraft 1.20.1 / Forge 47.4.0 instance.
3. Copy living-frontier-1.20.1-forge-0.2.0-beta.8.jar into THAT instance's
   mods folder. Fully close and restart Minecraft using that Forge profile.
4. Open the main-menu Mods screen and look for Living Frontier.
5. Start a new normal Overworld test world, with cheats enabled.

No Fabric API, Fabric Loader, GeckoLib or other content mod is required.
This JAR is NOT compatible with Fabric, NeoForge, Bedrock or Minecraft 26.3.
On a multiplayer server, install this same JAR on both server and clients.
Use Java 17. Your launcher may manage the required game runtime for you.
Source ZIPs and sources JARs are not installable mods.

The old Living Frontier 26.3 Fabric JAR is a DIFFERENT build. Do not place
it into this Forge instance. Do not try to downgrade a 26.3 world to 1.20.1.
Always back up saves before adding or removing mods. In an existing
1.20.1 world, the new bases appear only in newly generated chunks.
This project does not change your installed Minecraft profiles.
When upgrading from an earlier beta, remove its JAR before copying Beta 8.
Do not keep both versions installed. New mobs/encounters work in existing
1.20.1 saves; back up the save first. Structures still require new chunks.

WHAT IS INCLUDED
- Deer: herds in forests, taiga, plains and meadows. They flee raiders and
  non-sneaking survival players. Wheat and sweet berries attract/breed them.
- Songbirds: small flying/perching birds in forests and grassy biomes,
  breeding with wheat seeds or melon seeds.
- Fireflies: small hovering groups in swamps and selected forests at
  night. They glow visually; they do not illuminate surrounding blocks.
- Wild Boars: neutral roaming herds, retaliating together if attacked.
  Breed with carrots, potatoes or beetroot. Health: 24 (12 hearts).
- Forest Prowlers: fast hostile night packs in forests/taiga. They hunt
  players and deer and roam together. Health: 20 (10 hearts).
- Sky Wraiths: flying night hostiles circling before a warning flap and
  diving attack, with recovery between swoops. Health: 18 (9 hearts).
  Their glow is visual only, not dynamic lighting.
- Travellers: peaceful wandering people in forests, taiga, plains, savanna
  and meadows, alongside existing wildlife. Backpack/cap/cloak models,
  conversations with local lore, avoidance of nearby hostiles, and a preference
  for nearby wilderness-road waypoints while roaming.
  Health: 20 (10 hearts). Roaming travellers can despawn; village visitors
  have saved homes and remain with their village.
- Village Guards: neutral melee and bow defenders with 32 health
  (16 hearts). They patrol near the bell and defend against hostile mobs,
  excluding creepers, or retaliate when attacked. They do not replace
  vanilla iron golems or villagers. Shields are visual, not a blocking system.
- Biome settlements: five fortified village centres combining themed
  materials, medieval defenses, rustic homes/farms and fantasy landmarks
  with a market square. Only newly generated normal villages are redesigned.
  Existing villages and the rare abandoned variants are left intact.
- Wilderness trade roads: deterministic crossroads send roads in seeded random
  directions without locating or targeting villages. Roads follow local
  terrain, use biome-aware surfaces, avoid structures and stop at oceans.
  Viable road cells include one small camp and one two-house hamlet.
- Road traders: persistent merchants carry physical cargo between paired
  camps/hamlets. Right-click opens the vanilla merchant screen. Settlement
  stock, imports, exports and scarcity pricing persist with the world.
  Newly discovered inhabited villages join the same economy and send a trader
  toward the nearest roadside settlement; roads themselves never target them.
- Frontier Raiders: uncommon surface-spawning bands of 3-5. Band members
  follow a nearby leader and cooperate in combat. They do not burn in
  sunlight, break blocks or target villagers.
- Raider camps: redesigned 31x31 palisade encampments with varied canvas
  tents, roofed watchtowers, supply sheds, workshop details, paths, banners,
  a campfire gathering area, seven loot chests and six guards.
  The two tower guards are archers; the four ground guards are melee.
  Random placement grid spacing: 28 chunks.
- Ruined keeps: redesigned 57x57 weathered fortresses with outer towers,
  entrance foyer, an enclosed roofed maze, guarded rune rooms, dead ends,
  nine loot chests, nine guards and one sealed Frontier Warlord.
  Three fortress guards are archers, six are melee.
  Random placement grid spacing: 52 chunks.
- Frontier Warlord: 160 health, heavy melee attacks and a ground slam.
  Raised weapon, metallic sound and particles give a 1.5-second warning.
  Retreat beyond four blocks. Below half health, slams happen more often.
  Drops include diamonds, emeralds, golden apples and a sentry trim.

The generated guards/boss have a home, persist across saves and do not
automatically respawn after defeat. Roaming raiders use normal hostile
despawning and mob caps. Peaceful difficulty removes hostile inhabitants;
switching back will not recreate them in existing bases.
Structure spacing is a placement grid, not a guaranteed distance between
bases. Candidate placements near villages are excluded.

MIXED RANGED AND MELEE GUARDS
Archers use bows and actual arrows, with a visible draw before firing.
They stop to aim at visible targets within 18 blocks and try to retreat
when opponents approach. Inside four blocks, they switch to modeled
melee weapons and ordinary melee attacks rather than shooting point-blank.
Newly added villages alternate melee/archer defenders: the default two
guards consist of one swordsman and one archer. Newly generated camps
have two archers/four melee; new keeps have three archers/six melee.
Raider encounter bands also alternate roles when they have two or more
members. Naturally spawned raiders/eggs choose archery about one third
of the time; village guard eggs choose either role.
Roles are saved and synced to clients. Existing guards without a saved
role receive a stable UUID-based assignment on first load; no new base
is required to give old guards access to ranged combat, but their old
population is not rearranged into the exact new-template ratio.
Health is unchanged: raiders/base guards 26 points, village guards 32,
Warlord 160. The Warlord keeps his melee weapon and slam; never a bow.

Village archers hold fire if a friendly creature/villager blocks the
firing line. Their arrows ignore friendly residents/creatures and
unintended players. Guards may still retaliate against a player who
deliberately attacks them. Hostile raider arrows ignore other raiders
and the Warlord. Arrow safety persists after saving/loading.
Guard arrows cannot be picked up, and managed bows are not bonus loot.
Vanilla armor/projectile damage mechanics still apply; no homing arrows
or block-destroying projectiles are used. Melee weapons/shields are model
geometry, not equipped gear bonuses or a shield-blocking simulation.

FORTRESS MAZE AND THREE-SEAL PUZZLES
Enter through the main gate and read the signs. Follow the maze to three
guarded side rooms: Dawn, River and Root. Each has three rune stones.
SUN is gold, LEAF is mossy stone, WAVE is blue lapis.
Right-click the room's stones in the hinted order:
  Dawn: SUN -> LEAF -> WAVE
  River: WAVE -> SUN -> LEAF
  Root: LEAF -> WAVE -> SUN
These are rune-order puzzles, not item/key consumption puzzles.
A wrong input resets only that room's unfinished sequence. Completed
rooms remain saved checkpoints. Players cooperate on the SAME fortress
progress; all seals and partial sequences survive world saves/restarts.
Return to the dark seal-controller plinth beside the final portcullis.
Right-click it for status. Sneak-right-click resets unfinished sequences
without losing completed rooms. Completing all three seals opens the
three-wide portcullis and awakens the existing boss; no extra boss spawns.
Supply chests and room signs offer checkpoints and navigation clues.
The enclosed shortest entrance-to-boss route is 158 walking steps with
13 turns, before detours to the side-room puzzles and treasure.

The boss is dormant and cannot take ordinary damage before all seals are
complete. Digging around walls does not bypass that requirement. Ordinary
fortress walls remain mineable; rune stones, controller and portcullis
blocks are unbreakable in Survival. Creative/commands can remove them.
Standalone Warlords summoned with an egg or /summon are NOT seal-locked.
Admin /kill remains available for cleanup. Defeated bosses never respawn.
There is no TNT, lava trap or destructive wrong-answer penalty.
Creative puzzle blocks are available in Functional Blocks, but placing
individual stones does not bind a new puzzle: the generated structure's
block-entity data supplies the room/controller links.

UPGRADING STRUCTURES
New designs generate only in NEW chunks or new worlds. Existing camps
and keeps are not overwritten, and their old bosses remain playable.
In a throwaway test world, /place structure can create the new designs,
including their guards and seals, but can overwrite nearby buildings.
Do not remove/downgrade the mod from a save containing its puzzle blocks
without a backup; older builds do not know these new block types.

RANDOM EXPLORATION ENCOUNTERS
While exploring the Overworld, occasional raider bands, prowler packs
or flying wraith groups may appear at a distance. Prowlers and wraiths
are nighttime encounters. A short action-bar message warns you.
Default cooldown is 6-12 in-game minutes per player, including the first
encounter; moving at least 32 blocks from the saved starting/last-attempt
position is required. Checks happen every 30 seconds. The cooldown is
saved with the world and is not reset by reconnecting/restarting.
These are bounded extra encounters, separate from ordinary natural
spawning. Defaults: at most six nearby roaming Frontier hostiles within
96 blocks and 24 living encounter mobs loaded in the Overworld.
The ordinary loaded-monster cap is also respected.
No extra encounters for Creative/Spectator/FakePlayers, Peaceful, disabled
mob spawning, underwater/underground/roofed players, lit positions,
near villages or within 96 blocks of generated guards.
Forest leaf canopies are treated as outdoors. New mobs spawn at least
24 blocks from players, only in loaded chunks, never inside solid blocks.
Turning an encounter species' spawn weight to zero also excludes that
species from extra encounters. Existing mobs/bases are not removed.

BIOME SETTLEMENT DESIGNS
Normal newly generated villages start from an original 41x41 market core:
  Plains: oak/white terracotta cottages and a lantern bell tower.
  Desert: sandstone courtyard homes and an oasis sun obelisk.
  Savanna: acacia/orange terracotta homes and an amber wind spire.
  Taiga: spruce/mossy stone cottages and a forest rune shrine.
  Snowy: insulated timber homes, covered lit farms and a frost beacon.
Each combines four wide open road gateways, perimeter walls/palisades,
two ladder-access watchtowers, two cottages with four beds, two irrigated
farms, four striped canopy stalls, vanilla job sites, house loot and a bell.
Landmarks are decorative vanilla-block architecture, not magical machines.
The defensive wall surrounds the market core, not all outer village houses.

Vanilla roads and their normal outer houses/profession buildings continue
growing from the gateways. Villagers, golems, cats and desert camels use
their ordinary vanilla generation pools. Normal trading and iron golem
behavior are unchanged. Rare abandoned villages retain their original
centres, processors and relative weights; they are not made into safe towns.

The redesign only changes the five vanilla town-centre generation pools.
Saved village starts retain their original templates, and existing blocks
are not replaced. Explore previously ungenerated terrain or a new world.
Vanilla /locate structure minecraft:village_plains (or village_desert,
village_savanna, village_taiga, village_snowy) still works, but may locate
an old village. In a disposable test world, /place structure
minecraft:village_plains creates a full generated village with the new
centre unless the rare abandoned variant is selected.
WARNING: /place writes blocks and can overwrite terrain or buildings.
Datapacks/mods overriding the same town-centre pools may take priority.

VILLAGE LIFE
When you approach an inhabited Overworld village, villagers' remembered
meeting bells are checked every ten seconds. A valid bell with at least
two adult villagers gains up to two guards, one traveller visitor and one
road trader when the trade network is enabled,
only if there are safe loaded-ground positions nearby. Paved squares and
snowy village ground qualify for residents too.
Where a flat, completely empty natural-ground plot is available, one
five-by-five canopy stall is added, with a barrel, composter, fletching
table and hanging lantern. These are normal vanilla job-site blocks
that villagers can use. No existing houses/chests/trees are overwritten.
The stall does not create extra vanilla villagers or replace their brains.
This small-stall upgrade is separate from the baked-in biome market cores;
it does not redesign existing villages or change their vanilla houses.
Dense/hilly villages may gain residents without a stall. Guards/visitors
are added once; moving the bell nearby or killing them does not create
replacement residents. Existing player-built villages with a claimed
bell and villagers can qualify. Turn villageLife.markets=false before
visiting villages if you want no decorative block placement.

WILDERNESS ROADS AND SETTLEMENT TRADE
Roads are generated from the world seed in independent road cells. Each viable
cell has a jittered crossroad and up to four roads extending in random seeded
directions. They are unrelated to vanilla village locations. New-chunk terrain
grading creates three-wide paths, biome surfaces and local shallow bridges;
known structure pieces are protected.

The two best roads in a viable cell gain a camp and a small hamlet. Camps have
tents, beds, supplies, a campfire and lanterns. Hamlets have two furnished
houses, a bell, workstations and stores. When a player reaches a completed
site, its residents are initialized once. Killing them does not respawn them.

Hamlets dispatch persistent road traders to their paired camps. Traders carry
an eight-slot cargo inventory, pause when the next chunk is unloaded, and
resume from saved waypoints later. Delivered goods increase local stock;
traders reload with local surplus goods. Low-stock goods cost more, abundant
goods cost less, and settlements publish purchase orders for scarce goods.
Player purchases remove physical cargo; goods sold to a trader join its cargo.
Right-click a road trader for the normal Minecraft merchant screen.
Sneak-right-click shows the trader's saved settlement route.

Roads, camps and hamlets appear only in chunks first generated with Beta 8.
Already generated Beta 7 road chunks are not rewritten. Villages already
recorded by the older one-time village-life upgrade are not given replacement
traders automatically.

VILLAGER CONVERSATIONS AND ERRANDS
Normal right-click still opens vanilla trading.
Sneak-right-click an adult villager for a profession-specific greeting and
a supply request. Hold the requested amount and sneak-right-click THAT
villager again to deliver it. The reward is two emeralds and a modest
positive vanilla gossip/reputation change. Helping may influence normal
trade pricing; no guaranteed discount or separate reputation UI is added.
Farmers: 12 wheat. Librarians: 8 paper. Smiths: 3 iron ingots.
Fletchers: 16 sticks. Other professions: 4 bread.
Only one rewarded errand per player per world game-day across ALL villages.
Requests expire on the next game-day; completing one is saved, so reconnects
or changing the daylight clock do not bypass the daily reward limit.
Creative conversation is greeting-only and pays no emeralds.
Baby villagers, spectators and badly disliked players do not receive jobs.
Right-click a traveller for short local lore, or a guard for a status line.
Road traders use Minecraft's standard merchant GUI; no custom screen is added.

CHECK IN-GAME
Find Living Frontier in the Forge Mods menu and its eleven spawn eggs in
the Creative Spawn Eggs tab.

  /summon livingfrontier:deer ~ ~ ~
  /summon livingfrontier:songbird ~ ~ ~
  /summon livingfrontier:firefly ~ ~ ~
  /summon livingfrontier:raider ~ ~ ~
  /summon livingfrontier:warlord ~ ~ ~
  /summon livingfrontier:boar ~ ~ ~
  /summon livingfrontier:prowler ~ ~ ~
  /summon livingfrontier:sky_wraith ~ ~ ~
  /summon livingfrontier:traveller ~ ~ ~
  /summon livingfrontier:trader ~ ~ ~
  /summon livingfrontier:village_guard ~ ~ ~

In a normal Overworld world with Generate Structures enabled:
  /locate structure livingfrontier:raider_camp
  /locate structure livingfrontier:ruined_keep

These commands can deliberately generate a base in a throwaway world:
  /place structure livingfrontier:raider_camp
  /place structure livingfrontier:ruined_keep
WARNING: /place writes blocks, can overwrite terrain/buildings and creates
new inhabitants on every invocation. Do not use near anything valuable.

If a command is unknown, check cheats/permissions and the Forge Mods menu.
If a structure cannot be found, check Overworld, normal world generation,
Generate Structures and whether you are searching already generated land.

CONFIG
Forge creates config/livingfrontier-common.toml on first launch.
Natural spawning weights in its spawning section default to:
  deerWeight = 8
  songbirdWeight = 10
  fireflyWeight = 12
  raiderWeight = 4
  boarWeight = 6
  prowlerWeight = 5
  skyWraithWeight = 3
  travellerWeight = 3
Each accepts 0..100. Zero disables natural spawning of that species.
Weights are relative chances, not mobs per minute. Restart after edits.
Disabling raider natural spawns does not remove base inhabitants.

The encounters section controls extra encounters:
  enabled = true
  minMinutes = 6
  maxMinutes = 12
  localCap = 6
  globalCap = 24
Set enabled=false to keep normal mob spawning without the extra events.
Restart after edits. A maxMinutes below minMinutes is clamped to minMinutes.
Changing intervals does not replace already saved cooldown deadlines.

The villageLife section controls village upgrades and conversations:
  enabled = true
  markets = true
  conversations = true
  guardsPerVillage = 2
enabled=false stops automatic village additions, not existing residents.
markets=false prevents new stalls; existing stalls remain.
This option only controls automatic small-stall upgrades; the new worldgen
market designs are part of the village templates, not runtime additions.
conversations=false disables sneak-click villager errands, not trading.
guardsPerVillage accepts 0..4 and applies only to newly discovered villages.
Restart after changing these options. Traveller natural spawning is
controlled separately by spawning.travellerWeight (zero disables it).

The wildernessRoads section controls new-chunk roads and trade sites:
  enabled = true
  regionSize = 384
  spurLength = 224
  roadsideSites = true
  tradeNetwork = true
regionSize controls approximate crossroad spacing (256..768 blocks).
spurLength controls random road reach (96..512 blocks).
roadsideSites=false keeps roads but removes new camps/hamlets.
tradeNetwork=false keeps generated sites but adds no roadside/village traders.
These options do not rebuild saved chunks or replace existing populations.

BETA LIMITATIONS
This is a biome-settlement beta, not a finished public-release balance pass.
Original models/textures are included; vanilla sound events are reused.
Firefly glow is visual only. Camp/fortress designs currently have one variant each.
Village cores have one original layout per biome; outer houses stay vanilla.
The source includes Forge GameTests for entity registration, biome spawns,
generated roots, normal-world candidates, inhabited templates and boss
persistence/loot, encounter cooldowns/caps and actual flying swoop damage.
Village coverage includes once-only residents, safe stall placement and
saved daily rewards/costs/reputation, with ordinary trading preserved.
Trade-network coverage includes deterministic road/site pairing, continuous
merchant routes, one-time camp population, saved cargo/stock, transfers,
scarcity pricing and vanilla merchant offers.
Puzzle coverage includes maze reachability, wrong-input resets, checkpoints,
cooperative completion, locked/awakened boss damage and rotated/mirrored
template bindings. Interactive visual and survival difficulty tuning remain.
Guard coverage includes saved roles/loadouts, actual ranged hits, close
melee fallback and no-fire behavior behind villagers, plus bow-model poses.
Village design coverage includes all five palettes, usable beds/bells/job
sites/farms/loot, actual native inhabitants, paved/snowy guard placement,
vanilla street/house expansion and saved vanilla/new template compatibility.
Hands-on client rendering and multiplayer/balance
playtesting are still required. Back up worlds and report issues with the
Minecraft version, Forge version, mod version and relevant latest.log lines.
Do not include access tokens, personal paths or unrelated log details.

SOURCE AND BUILD
Main Java: src/main/java/dev/livingfrontier
Client models/renderers: src/main/java/dev/livingfrontier/client
World generation and assets: src/main/resources
Integration tests: src/gametest

Requires a Java 17 JDK. On Windows, set JAVA_HOME and run:
  .\Build.ps1
The script builds outside OneDrive's synchronized output folders, runs
Forge GameTests and copies the reobfuscated installable JAR into dist.
A project-local .tools JDK, when present, is used instead of JAVA_HOME.
No local JDK, Gradle caches or Minecraft binaries are in the source ZIP.

Standard Gradle:
  .\gradlew.bat build
  .\gradlew.bat runGameTestServer
  .\gradlew.bat runClient
On Linux/macOS use ./gradlew instead. The Gradle wrapper downloads its own
Gradle distribution. ForgeGradle prepares the Minecraft dependencies.
Development game instances are separate from your installed launcher.
runGameTestServer clears only its generated development test world at the
start of each run, so previous fixture mobs cannot contaminate mob caps.

Deterministic asset generators (Node.js, no npm dependencies):
  node tools/generate-world.mjs
  node tools/generate-textures.mjs
The world generator includes tools/generate-villages.mjs, which can also
run independently to rebuild/check just the biome village resources.
Generated resources are checked in, so Node is not needed to build/play.
Change the generator when editing assets that it regenerates.
